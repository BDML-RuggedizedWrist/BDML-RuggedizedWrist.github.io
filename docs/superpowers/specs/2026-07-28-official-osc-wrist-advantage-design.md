# Official IsaacLab OSC Wrist-Advantage Design

## Objective

Demonstrate, in a side-by-side Isaac Sim scene, why the Flexiv Rizon 4s
benefits from the additional two-degree-of-freedom wrist during ultrasound
scanning and fixed-point probe reorientation.

The red robot uses the original seven Rizon joints with the supplemental
wrist locked. The green robot uses all nine joints. Both robots receive the
same operational-space pose, wrench, and stiffness commands. During each
fixed-point reorientation, the green robot's first seven joints must travel
at least 50 percent less than the red robot's first seven joints.

## Controller Boundary

All task-space torque generation comes directly from
`isaaclab.controllers.OperationalSpaceController`. The project must not
implement a pseudoinverse, operational-space inertia matrix, Jacobian
transpose force term, null-space projector, or an alternative OSC solver.

The controller data flow follows IsaacLab's
`scripts/tutorials/05_controllers/run_osc.py`:

1. Read the PhysX end-effector Jacobian, generalized mass matrix, gravity
   compensation vector, pose, velocity, contact force, joint position, and
   joint velocity.
2. Express the Jacobian, pose, velocity, task frame, and force in the robot
   root frame.
3. Construct a `pose_abs + wrench_abs + variable_kp` command.
4. Call `OperationalSpaceController.set_command()`.
5. Call `OperationalSpaceController.compute()` with the complete dynamics
   state and the null-space joint-position target.
6. Apply only the returned joint efforts, subject to the robot's physical
   effort and effort-rate limits.

The hybrid controller configuration uses:

- `target_types=["pose_abs", "wrench_abs"]`
- `impedance_mode="variable_kp"`
- `inertial_dynamics_decoupling=True`
- `partial_inertial_dynamics_decoupling=False`
- `motion_damping_ratio_task=1.0`
- `motion_control_axes_task=[1, 1, 0, 1, 1, 1]`
- `contact_wrench_control_axes_task=[0, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0]`
- `nullspace_control="position"`

The patient-normal task axis remains force-controlled. The two surface
tangent axes and all three orientation axes remain motion-controlled.
Robot gravity remains enabled, so the controller's built-in gravity
compensation is enabled and receives the PhysX gravity vector. This is the
only deliberate configuration difference from the tutorial, whose Franka
example disables robot gravity.

The approach phase uses a second official
`OperationalSpaceController` configured for six-axis pose control. It uses
the same full inertial decoupling, variable stiffness, and built-in
null-space implementation. Switching controllers does not introduce any
locally computed task-space torque.

## Actuation Boundary

The active joints follow the effort-controlled actuator setup in IsaacLab's
official OSC tutorial. Their implicit actuator position stiffness and
velocity damping are both exactly zero:

- red joints 1 through 7: stiffness `0`, damping `0`;
- green joints 1 through 7: stiffness `0`, damping `0`;
- green supplemental wrist joints 8 and 9: stiffness `0`, damping `0`.

These values apply under both the pose-only approach controller and the
hybrid force/motion controller. Thus no implicit joint drive competes with
the efforts returned by `OperationalSpaceController`.

The only exception is the red robot's supplemental wrist. Those two joints
are outside the red OSC joint set and remain position-locked at zero with
stiffness `45` and damping `7`. The lock makes the red mechanism physically
seven-degree-of-freedom; it is not a second controller acting on any of the
red robot's seven active joints.

## Redundancy Policy

The red controller receives only Rizon joints 1 through 7. Its supplemental
wrist is locked at zero with the existing position drive.

The green controller receives all nine joints. Immediately after the
articulation reset state has been applied and synchronized at run
initialization, the program measures `q8_initial` and `q9_initial` once.
These two values are immutable for the rest of the run; phase changes never
replace them.

At the start of every phase, the program measures and stores that phase's
joints-1-through-7 positions. On every control step in the phase, the exact
null-space position target passed to the official controller is:

```text
q_null[J1..J7] = q_phase_start[J1..J7]
q_null[J8]     = q8_initial - relative_pitch
q_null[J9]     = q9_initial + relative_yaw
```

`relative_pitch` and `relative_yaw` are the signed task-frame reorientation
commands generated by the trajectory, in radians. A pitch-only phase has
`relative_yaw = 0`; a yaw-only phase has `relative_pitch = 0`. Every scan,
transit, force-ramp, return-neutral, and other non-reorientation phase sets
both relative angles to zero, so both wrist targets return to their
run-initial baselines.

The wrist null targets must never be copied from current wrist position or
from wrist position at a phase boundary. Either choice would turn an
accumulated offset into the next phase's baseline and allow a staircase
drift across scan, pitch, return, yaw, and challenge phases.

The wrist-axis signs are verified from the simulated Jacobian columns and a
small positive joint-displacement test before the challenge sequence is
accepted. The final signs are constants in the checked-in configuration;
there is no run-time trial-and-error controller.

The null-space stiffness is IsaacLab's default value of 10.0 for both
robots. A separate high-gain controller for the green robot is not allowed.

This policy only supplies `nullspace_joint_pos_target` to
`isaaclab.controllers.OperationalSpaceController.compute()`. It does not
compute or add joint efforts, directly position-control the green wrist,
alter the primary pose/wrench command, or implement a pseudoinverse,
Jacobian projector, weighted solver, or custom null-space controller. The
official controller remains free to project this posture preference under
the higher-priority operational-space task.

### Diagnostic basis for the run-initial wrist baseline

A 2,300-step torque-chain diagnostic isolated the official task torque from
the official null-space torque and the project's post-controller effort and
effort-rate clamps. During the surface scan, green J9 moved from `0.319` rad
to its `1.571` rad upper limit. The subsequent yaw phase began at `1.556`
rad and again reached the limit, despite a J9 null-to-task RMS torque ratio
of only `2.70e-7` in yaw (`4.56e-5` during scan). J9 effort-clamp and
effort-rate-clamp fractions were both `0.0`. Therefore the limit approach
was driven by the official primary operational-space task and then
constrained by the physics position/velocity limits; it was not caused by a
custom clamp or a direct wrist controller.

The same diagnostic showed arm and wrist angular-velocity contributions
opposing each other in `96.2%` of surface-scan samples, `80.0%` of
pitch-only samples, `91.2%` of return-pitch samples, and `61.7%` of
yaw-only samples. This counter-motion explains why an unconstrained
redundant primary solution can consume wrist range without producing a
matching end-effector rotation. Anchoring non-reorientation phases to the
single run-initial wrist baseline prevents each phase from ratifying the
previous phase's drift, while the signed pitch/yaw offsets express the
desired distal-wrist preference through the official null-space interface.
Because null-space projection can attenuate that preference, the result
must be accepted from simulation measurements rather than assumed from the
target formula.

## Demonstration Sequence

The default GUI run contains two examples after approach and force ramp.
The probe remains in contact and the target normal wrench remains 15 N
throughout both examples.

### Example 1: Equal-accuracy motion comparison

1. Scan 160 mm along the upper torso in 4 seconds while keeping the probe
   acoustic axis normal to the surface.
2. At the scan endpoint, rotate only task-frame pitch from 0 to 35 degrees.
3. Return pitch to zero.
4. Rotate only task-frame yaw from 0 to 45 degrees.
5. Return yaw to zero.

Both robots must complete this example without non-probe patient collision.
For both the pitch-only and yaw-only phases, the green robot's cumulative
absolute travel over joints 1 through 7 must be no more than 50 percent of
the red robot's corresponding travel. End-effector pose and force accuracy
must be comparable before the reduction is reported.

### Example 2: Near-patient collision challenge

The common probe target moves to Assembly3 torso surface coordinates
`x=0.10 m, y=1.32 m`, near the shoulder-side boundary of the validated
surface map. At that fixed point, pitch alone changes from 0 to 50 degrees
and returns to zero.

The green robot must complete the pose while maintaining contact through
the distal wrist. The red robot is expected either to:

- trigger a non-probe robot-to-patient collision, or
- exceed twice the green robot's joints-1-through-7 travel.

If the red robot triggers a collision, its challenge trajectory freezes at
the last safe reference and its status becomes `COLLISION_STOP`. The green
robot continues to the common requested goal. Physics collision response
stays enabled; visual interpenetration is not used as evidence.

The challenge point and 50-degree angle are fixed test inputs. A result that
does not meet the stated collision-or-motion criterion is a failed
demonstration, not a reason to silently move the target during the run.

## Collision Classification

Probe-to-patient contact is intentional and is measured by the existing
probe contact sensors.

Additional contact sensors cover every robot rigid body except
`linear_probe`. Their patient-filtered contact histories classify unwanted
arm contact. A non-probe collision is latched when patient contact exceeds
2 N. Contacts with the pedestal, ground, or the robot itself do not count as
patient collisions.

The GUI displays each side independently:

- `CONTACT OK`
- `NEAR COLLISION` for a non-probe patient contact between 0.5 N and 2 N
- `COLLISION STOP` after the 2 N latch

## Metrics and Visual Evidence

Metrics are accumulated separately for every phase instead of relying only
on whole-run cumulative travel.

Each phase records:

- cumulative absolute travel of joints 1 through 7 for red and green;
- cumulative absolute travel of green wrist joints 8 and 9;
- green-to-red main-arm travel ratio and reduction percentage;
- maximum end-effector position and orientation error per side;
- maximum and root-mean-square 15 N force error per side after settling;
- maximum contact-loss duration per side;
- maximum non-probe patient-contact force per side;
- whether each side completed the requested phase;
- whether both sides received identical pose, wrench, stiffness, and task
  frame commands before a collision stop.

The screen overlays the current phase, force command and measurement,
phase-local arm travel, green wrist travel, validated reduction percentage,
and collision state. Reduction text remains hidden until both sides satisfy
the phase's accuracy gates.

The JSON report contains separate `equal_accuracy_comparison` and
`collision_challenge` sections. Whole-run cumulative travel remains
diagnostic only.

## Acceptance Criteria

The equal-accuracy comparison passes only when:

- both sides receive identical operational-space commands;
- both sides complete pitch-only and yaw-only phases;
- green joints 1 through 7 travel at least 50 percent less in each phase;
- force commands remain 15 N;
- post-settling measured force stays within 1.5 N of target;
- probe normal error during surface scanning stays below 3 degrees;
- task orientation error stays below 5 degrees;
- tangent position error stays below 10 mm;
- contact loss does not exceed 0.1 seconds;
- neither robot has a non-probe patient collision.

The collision challenge passes only when:

- green completes the 50-degree pitch goal;
- green maintains the same force, pose, and contact tolerances;
- green has no non-probe patient collision;
- red either enters `COLLISION_STOP` or its main-arm travel exceeds twice
  green's main-arm travel.

The complete demonstration passes only when both sections pass. The report
must preserve failed measurements and must not claim a wrist advantage when
the accuracy gates fail.

## Testing and Verification

Pure Python tests cover:

- official-controller source-contract checks;
- the exact tutorial-derived configuration;
- zero implicit stiffness and damping on every active effort-controlled
  joint, with only the red supplemental wrist retaining its `45/7` lock;
- one-time run-initial wrist baseline capture, persistence across phase
  boundaries, exact signed wrist targets, zero-relative-angle return to
  baseline, and phase-start joints-1-through-7 hold behavior;
- rejection of current-position or phase-start wrist targets;
- phase-local travel aggregation and the 50 percent gate;
- reduction hiding when accuracy differs;
- probe versus non-probe collision classification;
- collision-stop state transitions;
- the two fixed demonstration sequences.

After the pure tests pass, Scheme A first runs a short, 2,300-step GPU
validation with the existing trajectory, gains, limits, force command,
acceptance thresholds, and challenge coordinates unchanged. A validation
watchdog rejects the experiment and prevents the 3,500-step run if any of
the following occurs:

- either green wrist joint remains within `0.02` rad of an authored limit
  for at least `0.10` seconds;
- either green wrist joint remains at or above `1.99 rad/s` in absolute
  speed for at least `0.10` seconds;
- either probe loses required patient contact for more than `0.10` seconds;
- either probe's filtered measured normal-force magnitude exceeds `30 N`;
- green non-probe patient contact exceeds the existing `2 N` collision
  threshold, or either robot does so before the collision-challenge phase;
- for more than `0.25` consecutive seconds, a commanded translation
  advances by at least `1 mm` while the corresponding measured
  end-effector translation advances by less than `0.1 mm`, or a commanded
  rotation advances by at least `0.5` degrees while the corresponding
  measured end-effector rotation advances by less than `0.05` degrees;
- any controller input, output, state, or metric becomes non-finite, or the
  simulator/controller raises an error.

The consecutive-window rule is the task-freeze detector. It is evaluated
per robot and only on the translation or rotation channel whose command
exceeds its excitation threshold. It is disabled for red after an
intentional challenge `COLLISION_STOP`. Normal trajectory endpoint dwells
therefore cannot create a false freeze.

Only a short run with none of these stop conditions advances to the full
3,500-step GPU validation. The full run produces the JSON report and exits
nonzero unless all acceptance criteria pass. After headless validation, a
GUI run is started without `--max_steps`; it remains open until the user
closes the Isaac Sim window.

## Deferred Follow-On Trajectory

The user-approved implementation order is to validate Scheme A before
changing the clinical trajectory. The following sequence is therefore a
separate follow-on design and must not be mixed into Scheme A's tests,
2,300-step safety run, or 3,500-step acceptance report:

1. Move along the patient from the robot-near end to the robot-far end,
   settle at one fixed surface point, maintain `15 N`, and reorient there.
   Both robots share the transit; after arrival, green should express the
   reorientation primarily through joints 8 and 9 while red must use its
   main arm.
2. At the same point and with the same surface normal and `15 N` contact,
   rotate the probe exactly `90` degrees about its acoustic axis to switch
   between longitudinal and transverse image planes.
3. Move to a heart-region point nearer the patient's head, maintain `15 N`,
   and perform another fixed-point reorientation whose green
   joints-1-through-7 travel is smaller than red's.

The follow-on timing target is approximately `2.5x` the present motion
speed: the current `4.0`-second, `160 mm` scan becomes `1.6` seconds; a
`2.0`-second point transfer becomes `0.8` seconds; existing reorientation
durations are multiplied by `0.4`; and the acoustic-axis `90`-degree
rotation receives `0.8` seconds. Quintic time scaling, contact supervision,
collision handling, the official OSC boundary, and the `15 N` normal-force
command remain unchanged. These timings are follow-on inputs, not
parameters to tune while evaluating Scheme A.

## Non-Goals

- No custom operational-space solver or weighted pseudoinverse.
- No artificial reduction of the green robot's arm effort limits.
- No separate position controller that directly drives wrist joints 8 and
  9 during the comparison.
- No deformable-patient simulation.
- No claim of hardware or clinical validation.
