RuggedizedWrist

Two distal degrees of freedom for contact-rich tool reorientation

Biomimetics and Dexterous Manipulation Laboratory · Stanford University

Interactive CADSimulation videosSource code

A seven-axis robot can realize a six-dimensional end-effector pose, yet reorienting a contact tool may still require large motions of its long links. RuggedizedWrist adds two compact rotational axes at the tool. The simulations below compare the original 7-DoF arm with the 9-DoF system under the same operational-space pose and 15 N surface-normal force objectives.

Motivation

Why do we need two extra DoF?

Redundancy in the main arm does not guarantee a favorable posture near a patient. A moderate change in tool orientation can move the elbow through a large arc, approach a singular configuration, or reduce collision clearance.

Distal redundancy changes the distribution of motion. Once the probe reaches a contact point, the wrist can perform most of the local orientation change while the seven main joints stay close to their phase-start posture.

  • Smaller main-arm joint-space path length
  • Reduced swept volume near the patient
  • Local 90° imaging-plane changes without arm reorganization

Experiments

Matched-view Isaac Lab comparisons

Each pair uses the same side-view camera transform, scene geometry, motion timing, and playback clock. The recordings contain only the rendered simulation—no Isaac Lab interface or data overlay.

Fixed-point cardiac reorientation

After reaching the cardiac region, both systems maintain a 15 N surface-normal command while changing probe orientation. The 9-DoF system assigns most of the reorientation to the two distal wrist joints, reducing motion of the main arm.

Near-to-far longitudinal scan

The probe scans from the robot-near end of the torso to the far end, holds contact at a fixed point, changes one orientation axis at a time, and finally rotates 90° about the probe axis for a second imaging plane.
